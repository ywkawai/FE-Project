To represent cloud-microphysics processes, 
we provide two microphysics schemes using SCALE library: 
three-class one-moment bulk and six-class one-moment bulk schemes.

In addition, 
a large-scale condensation scheme can be used. 
It is useful to investigate a feedback mechanism between the equations of motion and moisture within simplified physics.

\subsection{Three-class one-moment bulk scheme}
%%
As a three-class one-moment bulk microphysics scheme, 
the Kessler parameterization \citep{kessler_1969} can be used. 
In this scheme, 
the distribution function of particle size is expressed only by mass concentration. 
Considering two categories of water in cloud and rain, 
the ratios of the densities of cloud and rain to total air density are calculated.

The details of formulation are found in Section 8.3.1 of SCALE description \citep{scale_library}.

\subsection{Six-class one-moment bulk scheme}
%%
A six-class one-moment bulk microphysics scheme is based on \citet{tomita_2008}. 
This scheme calculates mass exchange among six categories of water substances, 
which are water vapor, cloud water, rain, cloud ice, snow, and graupel.  
Although the scheme largely follows \citet{lin_etal_1983}, 
the original method of \citet{lin_etal_1983} is modified as follows: 
Both cloud water and cloud ice are generated only by a saturation adjustment process, 
and wet growth process of graupel is omitted.
\citet{tomita_2008} indicates that these modifications result in 20\% reduction in computational cost 
without significant changes on physical performance.

The details of formulation are found in Section 8.3.2 of SCALE description \citep{scale_library}.


\subsection{Large-scale condensation scheme}
%
If the moisture content exceeds its saturation value,
the excess water vapor is converted to cloud water.
The associated latent heat is released to the atmosphere 
and the cloud water is removed with a certain timescale.
A typical value of timescale is set to about O($10^3$) seconds when the horizontal grid spacing is O($10^2$) km.

Based on approaches in some AGCMs, such as the Integrated Forecasting System (IFS) of the European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts \citep{tiedtke1987parametrization}, 
the formulation is described as follows.
If the specific humidity exceeds the saturation value $q_{v,s}$,
specific humidity $q_v$ is reduced toward $q_{v,s}$. 
The excess water vapor is converted to cloud water, which is removed as precipitation.
The temperature is increased by the release of latent heat. 

During the moist adjustment, 
the moist internal energy is assumed to be conserved:
%%
\begin{align}
 \delta ( C_{v,{\rm tot}} T + L q_v + L_l q_c ) = 0,
\end{align}
%%
where $C_{v,{\rm tot}} = (1-q_v- q_c) C_{v,d} + q_v C_{v,v} + q_c C_{v,l}$, 
and $L$ and $L_l$ denote the latent heats of vaporization and fusion, respectively.
Following the definition adopted in the SCALE library, 
the moist internal energy is defined by taking liquid water as the reference state and, thus, $L_l=0$.
Since cloud water is produced by condensation of water vapor, 
$\delta q_c=-\delta q_v$. 
Retaining terms to first order in the adjustment increments, 
we obtain
%%
\begin{align}
C^*_{v,{\rm tot}} \delta T = [L + (C_{v,v} - C_{v,l})T^*]\delta q_c,
\label{eq:moist_adjustment_delta}
\end{align}
%%
where the single asterisks denote the unadjusted states.
The amount of condensed water is determined by the difference between the unadjusted specific humidity and the saturation specific humidity at the unadjusted temperature:
%%
\begin{align}
\delta q_c = q_v^* - q_{v,s}(T^* + \delta T).
\label{eq:delta_qc}
\end{align}
%%
Because the $q_{v,s}$ depends on temperature through the Clausius-Clapeyron equation, 
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:moist_adjustment_delta} and \eqref{eq:delta_qc} form an implicit system.
For idealized experiments, $q_{v,s}$ is often linearized around $T^*$ as
%%
\begin{align}
  q_{v,s}(T^*+\delta T) \approx q_{v,s}(T^*) + \dfrac{dq_{v,s}}{dT}\Big|_{T=T^*} \delta T.
\label{eq:delta_qc_linearized}
\end{align}
%%
Neglecting the pressure dependence during the adjustment,
the temperature derivative of the saturation specific humidity is approximated using the Clausius--Clapeyron relation as
%%
\begin{align*}
  \dfrac{dq_{v,s}}{dT} \simeq \dfrac{L q_{v,s}}{R_v T^2}.
\end{align*}
%%
Combining Eqs.~\eqref{eq:moist_adjustment_delta} and \eqref{eq:delta_qc_linearized} gives
%%
\begin{align}
%% \delta T = \dfrac{L + (C_{v,v}-C_{v,l})T^*}{C^*_{v,{\rm tot}} + [L + (C_{v,v}-C_{v,l})T^*] \frac{dq_{v,s}^*}{dT}} (q_v^* -q_{v,s}(T^*)), \\
 \delta q_c = C^*_{v,{\rm tot}} \dfrac{q_v^* -q_{v,s}(T^*)}{C^*_{v,{\rm tot}} + [L + (C_{v,v}-C_{v,l})T^*] \frac{dq_{v,s}^*}{dT}\Big|_{T=T^*}}.
\end{align}
%%
When a simplified moist thermodynamics is used, 
$C_{v,v}=C_{v,l}$ and $C^*_{v,{\rm tot}} \approx C_{v,d}$.
Then, $\delta q_c$ simplifies to
%%
\begin{align}
  \delta q_c = \dfrac{q_v^* - q_{v,s}(T^*)}{1 + \frac{L}{C_{v,d}} \frac{dq_{v,s}^*}{dT}\Big|_{T=T^*}}, 
\end{align}
%%
%which corresponds to Eq.\,(2) in \cite{thatcher2016}.
Here, $\delta q_c$ is introduced as an intermediate quantity representing the amount of condensed water.
The condensate is subsequently removed as precipitation and is not retained as prognostic cloud water in this scheme.

Finally, the condensation rate is defined as
%%
\begin{align*}
  C_{\rm rate} = \dfrac{\delta q_c}{\tau_{\rm LSC}}
\end{align*}
%%
where $\tau_{\rm LSC}$ is the prescribed adjustment timescale for the large-scale condensation process.
For the diabatic heating in the density-weighted potential temperature in Eq.\,\eqref{eq:diabatic_heating} 
and the source terms in the mass equations of water vapor, 
we have
\begin{align}
  Q_{\rm LSC} &= \rho L C_{\rm rate}, \\
  S_{\rho q_v,{\rm LSC}} &= - \rho C_{\rm rate}.
\end{align}
